========================================================================
WHAT THIS PROJECT IS — IN PLAIN ENGLISH
========================================================================

You built a robot assistant that solves Machine Learning problems by
itself, using a small AI model that runs entirely on your own computer
(no internet, no ChatGPT, no paid API).

Think of it like this:

    YOU ask a question
         |
         v
    THE BRAIN (a small local AI model called llama3.2, run by a program
    called Ollama) THINKS about what to do next
         |
         v
    THE HANDS (a Python file called ml_tools.py) actually DOES the work
    — loads a dataset, trains a model, tunes settings, etc.
         |
         v
    THE BRAIN looks at the result and decides the NEXT step
         |
         v
    ...this repeats until the brain says "I'm done, here's your answer"

This loop (Think -> Act -> Observe -> Think again) is called a "ReAct
agent". The controller script (react_agent.py) is the part that manages
this loop — it asks the brain what to do, runs the actual tool, and
feeds the real result back.


========================================================================
THE 3 MAIN PARTS OF THE PROJECT
========================================================================

1. ml_tools.py
   The "toolbox". Contains 6 tools the AI can call:
   - load_dataset_summary   -> tells you about a dataset (iris/wine/etc.)
   - train_sklearn_model    -> trains a Decision Tree / Random Forest / etc.
   - train_pytorch_mlp      -> trains a simple neural network
   - tune_hyperparameters   -> auto-tunes model settings
   - reduce_dimensionality  -> simplifies data (PCA)
   - train_deep_classifier  -> trains a fancier neural network

2. react_agent.py
   The "controller" / "manager". Talks to the AI brain, reads what tool
   it wants to use, runs that tool for real, and reports the real result
   back so the AI can decide the next step. This is where almost all of
   today's bug-fixing happened.

3. benchmark_runner.py
   Uses the agent to automatically test all 3 algorithms on 2 datasets
   (6 combinations total) and builds a results table.


========================================================================
WHERE THE PROBLEMS WERE (in the order we found them)
========================================================================

The AI model you're using (llama3.2, a small 3-billion-parameter model)
is fast and free, but it's not very "smart" compared to something like
ChatGPT. It kept making the same handful of mistakes. Here they are,
simplest first:

--- PROBLEM 1: The AI made up fake results instead of doing the work ---
Sometimes the AI would just WRITE what it thought the answer might look
like, without actually calling a tool. It's like a student writing
"the experiment showed 95% accuracy" without ever running the
experiment.
FIX: We made the controller smarter — if the AI says "I'm going to do
X" and THEN writes a final answer without doing X, we throw away the
fake final answer and force it to actually do X first.

--- PROBLEM 2: The AI got stuck repeating itself ---
The AI would call the same tool with the same input over and over
(like a broken record), instead of moving to the next step. Example:
it would ask "what does the wine dataset look like?" 5 times in a row.
FIX (this took several tries to properly diagnose):
  - First we tried: "if you repeat yourself, I'll remind you not to."
    This helped a little but not fully.
  - We tried making the AI's word choices more "random" to break the
    loop — this backfired badly and made the AI produce garbage text.
  - The REAL cause, which we eventually found by reading the raw data
    sent to the AI: every time the AI repeated itself, the controller
    was ALSO copying that repeated text back into what the AI reads
    next — so the AI was staring at its own mistake twice, which made
    it MORE likely to repeat it a third time, not less.
  FIX: Stop copying the repeated mistake back in. Only give the AI a
  short reminder message instead. This is the fix that actually worked.

--- PROBLEM 3: The AI invented numbers for work it never did ---
This was the most serious one. Once problem 2 was fixed, the AI got
further along — but then, once it had gathered SOME real results, it
would just invent believable-looking numbers for the rest instead of
finishing the remaining work. Like a student who did 1 out of 6 homework
questions, then just wrote plausible-looking answers for the other 5.
FIX: We added a hard rule in the controller: "you are NOT allowed to
give a final answer until I've personally verified that all 6 required
pieces of work were actually done for real." If the AI tries to finish
early, the controller rejects it and tells it exactly what's still
missing.

--- PROBLEM 4: Even without cheating, the AI couldn't reliably finish
    a long list of 6 tasks in one sitting ---
Even with cheating blocked, asking the small AI to do 6 things in a row
in one long conversation was unreliable — it would do 1-5 correctly,
then get confused and stall.
FIX: Instead of asking it to do all 6 in one long conversation, we broke
it into 6 SEPARATE short conversations — one per task. The small AI is
very reliable at doing ONE thing when asked directly; it just struggles
to track a long list. Then we glue the 6 real results together into the
final table ourselves (in code), so the table's numbers are always
100% real, and we only ask the AI to write the short summary/opinion
paragraph at the end (a much easier job).

--- PROBLEM 5: The computer running the AI kept crashing ---
A few times, the local AI server itself (Ollama) crashed mid-task —
nothing to do with the code, just the AI using too much of your
computer's memory/CPU during a long run.
FIX: No code fix possible for this — just restart it
(`docker compose up -d ollama`) and try again. Documented as a known
limitation in the report, since it's an honest real-world side effect
of running AI locally instead of using a paid cloud service.


========================================================================
THE BIG LESSON FROM ALL THIS
========================================================================

A small, free, local AI model is NOT as reliable as a big cloud AI. It
will:
  - occasionally lie/make things up if not double-checked,
  - occasionally get stuck repeating itself,
  - struggle with long multi-step tasks.

The fix for all of this was NOT "write a better instruction for the
AI" (we tried that first, it wasn't enough). The real fix was building
a stricter CONTROLLER around the AI that:
  - never fully trusts the AI's word,
  - double-checks that real work was actually done before accepting
    an answer,
  - and breaks big jobs into small, easy, verifiable pieces.

This is actually a good, realistic lesson for the report: it shows you
understand that autonomous AI agents need guardrails, not just good
prompts.
